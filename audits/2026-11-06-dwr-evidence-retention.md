# DWR historical evidence retention — candidate, not yet released

The 6 November operation audit documents loss of DWR last-good snapshots after two hours during upstream failures. Retain DWR snapshots for up to six hours as historical evidence, while the operational observation window stays at two hours. Cache API storage is best effort and per data center; it may evict earlier. [Cloudflare Cache API documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/cache/) confirms response Cache-Control controls retention and cache writes do not guarantee storage.

The DWR delivery wrapper retains recorded status, warnings and original retrieval/observation clocks; observations older than two hours, invalid or too far in the future are marked stale. Both normal/fallback reads and origin-only probes apply this rule. Generic feeds keep their existing two-hour retention. Independent review also identified a basin-model soil aggregation path that did not check observation time; the builder now excludes stale/old/invalid/future DWR soil records, so historical retention cannot affect current wetness/runoff estimates. Readiness probes still contact the origin and cannot turn an outage into availability by reading historical data.

A three-hour retained critical record cannot declare a current warning or a no-alert state; it remains visible in the full briefing with its age. A six-hour snapshot is the inclusive retention bound; expired/future/invalid snapshots are rejected. Failed refreshes do not overwrite old evidence or renew its clock. The longer Cache API lifetime starts with the next successful live write; existing two-hour cache entries are not silently renewed.

Tests first reproduced the two-hour evidence loss and7200-second cache header. Focused API tests/typecheck passed after implementation; final current-head CI, independent code/render review and automatic deployment/live checks remain required. No release or whole-system safety signoff is claimed here.
